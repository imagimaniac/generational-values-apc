## 02 — Time Budget Model

**What "parallel" actually means at 2 hours a day.** One honest correction to how we tagged tasks earlier: for a single person, most "parallel" tasks don't save clock time — they just mean the tasks don't block each other, so you can reorder or batch them. Real time-savings only happen when a task is *passive* — a journal sitting in review, a model training, an email waiting for a reply. Those are marked `[WAITING]` below, and that's exactly where Track C work gets slotted in without adding to your daily budget.

| Ledger item | Estimated hours | At 2 hrs/day |
|---|---|---|
| Track A — strengthen paper for submission | ~20 hrs | ~2 weeks |
| Track A — submission & conference prep | ~16 hrs | ~1.5 weeks |
| Track B — research targets, draft & send outreach | ~16 hrs | ~1.5 weeks |
| *Waiting time (journal/conference review, replies)* | 0 active hrs | 8–20 weeks calendar, used for Track C |
| Track C — one credit-risk/capital-markets project | ~60–70 hrs | ~7–8 weeks each |

> **The practical upshot:** Track A's active work fits in about 5 weeks. The following 8–20 weeks of journal/conference silence is not idle time — it's exactly when Track C projects should run, since neither track competes for the same 2-hour slot.

## 03 — Track A: Publishing Pathway

**From finished project to submitted paper.** Two realistic routes exist, and you don't have to pick only one: a **journal route** (slower, more prestigious, harder for an independent researcher to break into) and a **conference route** (faster feedback, genuinely open to independent and early-career researchers, good networking). Run both.

### Phase A1 — Strengthen for submission (~20 hrs)
- [ ] **Expand literature review** — Ground the paper in Mannheim, Inglehart's postmaterialism work, and the Bell & Jones HAPC methodology papers. `[SEQUENTIAL]` (4 hrs)
- [ ] **Formalize the methods section** — Write up the HAPC specification, identification strategy, and sensitivity checks in academic register. `[SEQUENTIAL]` (5 hrs)
- [ ] **Reformat to target journal's author guidelines** — Pick the template (see journal shortlist below) and match structure, citation style, word limits. `[SEQUENTIAL]` (3 hrs)
- [ ] **Internal rigor pass** — Re-check every claim against the age/period/cohort caveats — this is what separates it from pop-sociology. `[SEQUENTIAL]` (3 hrs)
- [ ] **Post as a preprint (SocArXiv)** — Free, social-science-specific preprint server — gets you a citable, dated public version before formal submission. `[PARALLEL — with methods + reformat]` (2 hrs)
- [ ] **Get 1–2 informal outside reads** — Share the preprint link with someone methodologically literate before formal submission — catches errors cheaply. `[PARALLEL]` (3 hrs)

### Phase A2 — Submit & wait (~16 hrs active)
- [ ] **Choose target journal** — Start with an open-access, methodologically friendly venue rather than a legacy gated journal — see shortlist below. `[SEQUENTIAL]` (2 hrs)
- [ ] **Write cover letter & submit** — State the contribution in 3–4 sentences; independent-researcher status is fine to disclose plainly. `[SEQUENTIAL — after journal choice]` (2 hrs)
- [ ] **Prepare a conference abstract (250–400 words)** — Same findings, conference-length format — see target conferences below. `[PARALLEL — with cover letter]` (3 hrs)
- [ ] **Submit to 1–2 conferences** — AAPOR and/or ESRA — check current call-for-papers deadlines, they're annual/biennial. `[SEQUENTIAL — after abstract]` (2 hrs)
- [ ] **Journal review period** — Typically 2–6 months depending on venue. Zero active hours required — this is exactly where Track C starts. `[WAITING]` (8–24 wks calendar)
- [ ] **Respond to reviewer feedback / revise & resubmit** — Budget a dedicated ~2-week window whenever feedback actually arrives. `[SEQUENTIAL — when feedback arrives]` (~7 hrs)

> **Target journal shortlist (open-access, methodologically rigorous, realistic for an independent researcher):** *Demographic Research* (Max Planck Institute for Demographic Research — open access, fast-ish turnaround, publishes APC work regularly), *Sociological Science* (open access, unusually quick review, explicitly welcomes rigorous work regardless of author affiliation), *Social Science Research*, *Quality & Quantity*. *Public Opinion Quarterly* and the *International Journal of Public Opinion Research* are strong but more competitive — good stretch targets for a second paper once you have one publication credit.

## 08 — Risks & Ground Rules

- **Don't let Track C creep into Track A's active weeks.** The whole point of the calendar is that they don't compete for the same 2 hours — mixing them mid-week usually means both move slower.
- **Weekly total matters more than daily rigidity.** Missing a day is fine if the week still nets ~10 hours — don't let one skipped day cascade into abandoning the plan.
- **Journal review timelines are unpredictable.** The calendar above is a reasonable estimate, not a guarantee — treat Track C's start date as flexible, triggered by "Track A submission done," not by a fixed calendar month.
- **Outreach replies will be sparse.** Based on typical response rates for cold academic/industry outreach, expect maybe 1–2 substantive replies out of every 8–10 sent — that's a normal, not disappointing, result.
